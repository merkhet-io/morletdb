# Related work: retrieval-based forecasting of a bearing health trajectory

This is a bounded review, not a survey. It was compiled on 2026-10-31. Each entry records what was actually read:

- **[M]**: methods and evaluation sections read (arXiv HTML or PDF).
- **[A]**: abstract or landing page only.
- **[B]**: bibliographic record only; content not verified.

The experiment under review forecasts log-RMS H minutes ahead on XJTU-SY using exact k-NN retrieval of offset-corrected windows from other bearings. Baselines are persistence and AR. Controls are random retrieval and shuffled continuations. Evaluation is leave-one-bearing-out. No RUL claim is made.

## 2. What our experiment is

| Component | Classification | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Forecasting by retrieving the k most similar past windows and averaging their continuations | **Established method** | RAFT (offset subtraction, top-m, weighted continuations); analog ensembles (Delle Monache 2013); method of analogues (Lorenz 1969, not read) |
| Retrieving from *other units'* run-to-failure histories | **Established method** | SBP lineage (Wang et al. 2008, not read; Zio & Di Maio 2010 via Eker et al. 2014); RAF's cross-series database |
| Offset correction (subtracting the last value) | **Established method** | RAFT; RAF aligns the retrieved last value to the query |
| Exact rather than approximate k-NN | **Established method** | RAFT computes exhaustive correlations; brute-force kNN is textbook |
| Log-RMS as the slow observable; persistence and AR baselines | **Established practice.** No specific source was read in this review. | — |
| Random-retrieval control | **Established** | RAFT ablation (random retrieval); Dimidov & Frank 2026 (random references) |
| Applying all of the above to XJTU-SY log-RMS trajectories, leave-one-bearing-out, short-horizon health-indicator forecasting rather than RUL | **Replication / re-test on a new dataset and target.** Forecasting the trajectory instead of RUL changes the target, not the method. LOBO is standard grouped cross-validation. | — |
| Shuffled-continuation control (keeps matched windows but breaks the window→future pairing) | **Possibly new as a reported control.** It did not appear in any source read here. The search was bounded, so novelty is not established. It is a methodological safeguard, not a method contribution. | — |
| Database-native provenance: each retrieved source window and its future interval read back exactly as the stored raw waveforms | **Possibly new as a systems property.** None of the forecasting papers read trace retrieved evidence back to raw sensor records. Analogue methods are traceable in principle (each member is a dated past observation), so the idea of an identifiable analogue is not new. What may be new is exact, queryable read-back of the underlying high-rate waveforms within the store. | — |

Net: the forecasting method is established. If the experiment is positive, its contribution is evidence that cross-bearing analogues carry short-horizon information on XJTU-SY beyond persistence, AR, random retrieval and shuffled pairing. A second contribution would be the provenance mechanism. A negative result is equally reportable as a re-test.

## 3. What the wavelet, token and operator papers do not address

WaveToken, MWT, WNO, MSWT and FourCastNet 3 concern *representation and architecture for learned models*. These are wavelet tokenisers for a pretrained forecaster, and wavelet or Morlet-parameterised kernels for neural operators on simulated or reanalysis fields. None of them retrieves past episodes as evidence. None evaluates on machinery degradation, uses cross-unit controls, or provides provenance for a forecast. They do not bear on whether nearest-neighbour continuations of a bearing's log-RMS beat persistence. They are background for MorletDB's wavelet representation, not for this experiment's question. FourCastNet 3's use of Morlet wavelets is limited to the filter basis of a trained convolution, as stated in the paper.
