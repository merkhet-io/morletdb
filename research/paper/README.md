# MorletDB research manuscript

**MorletDB: Recordings, Not Rows — Lag-Tolerant Waveform Retrieval in PostgreSQL**, version 0.11, a
technical report / preprint (ACM `sigconf` layout). Release package: [RELEASE.md](RELEASE.md). Journal plan: [JOURNAL_DECISION.md](JOURNAL_DECISION.md).

## What we found

1. **Shifted probes speed up the tested bounded-lag scorer at high recall.** On held-out seismic data,
   probe search recovered 97.7% of the exact top ten at a p95 of 57 ms, against 740 ms for the fastest
   exact path (paper Table 2; [`e1_heldout.json`](../../evaluation/consolidated/results/e1_heldout.json)).
2. **It works because zero-lag candidates rank by a different quantity.** A zero-lag shortlist misses
   most matches whose best lag is beyond ±5 values, whether taken exactly or from HNSW (R2-D, paper
   Figure 2); probes on a sparse lag grid cover them (paper Figure 3 and Table 3).
3. **It costs storage, index passes and maintenance.** The probe table and index add about one times the
   raw data, each query makes 13 index passes, filters that exclude a query's neighbours slow it down, and
   maintaining the index at commit wrote much more write-ahead log than the data grew (paper Sections 8–10;
   [`sizes_heldout.csv`](../../evaluation/consolidated/results/sizes_heldout.csv), D2, C2).
4. **Not shown:** other signal domains, diagnostic relevance for machines, cold caches, and stable
   operation beyond 111 minutes (the planned two-hour run stopped early; paper Section 12).

## What changed in 0.11 (correctness and release pass; no new measurement)

- **Visibility of new data corrected.** Appending a chunk does not build envelopes; building is an
  explicit call (`morlet_build_envelopes`). Samples and their representations become visible together only
  when the caller runs append and builder in one transaction; probe search additionally needs probes
  enabled and probe rows present, and one builder call is bounded (`max_windows`), so it may not cover
  every window. No claim is made that a multi-statement probe search is atomic under READ COMMITTED.
  Corrected in the contributions, Figure 1 and its caption, Section 4, the BL1-S replay (Section 10),
  related work and the conclusion.
- **Kinds of evidence separated:** confirmatory studies (frozen protocol and decision rule), exploratory
  diagnostics and post hoc analyses are named in the main text, the supplement's study overview and
  [STUDIES.md](STUDIES.md); the main text states that E1 re-measured the held-out data already used by
  BL1-C and is not a new confirmation. Protocols and recorded deviations are unchanged.
- **Artifact:** [ARTIFACT.md](ARTIFACT.md) rewritten for a fresh checkout (environments, PostgreSQL,
  pgrx and pgvector, four separate procedures, stable commits instead of branch and PR references).
- **Release package:** version 0.11 throughout; [RELEASE_MANIFEST.json](RELEASE_MANIFEST.json) with PDF
  hashes; proposed report licence (CC BY 4.0, not applied:
  [LICENSE-REPORT-PROPOSAL.md](LICENSE-REPORT-PROPOSAL.md)); website copies of both PDFs checked for byte
  equality in CI.
- **Shorter abstract**; supplement bibliography no longer leaves a nearly empty last page.

## What changed in draft 0.7

A focused revision around one question: how bounded-lag waveform search can use standard vector
indexes while keeping the link to the recording, filter semantics and transactional updates, and which
costs limit it.

- **Argument and contributions.** Six-step argument (need → zero-lag fails → R2-D isolates the cause →
  shifted probes target it → integration in PostgreSQL → costs). Three contributions: a specific
  candidate-generation method, its recording-oriented integration, and a controlled evaluation.
- **Novelty.** New section "Related work and what is new" with a comparison table. Query-side shifting
  (Rafiei 1999; Keogh et al. 2006/2009), multi-query union with exact re-ranking (ColBERT), ANN
  candidates for seismic cross-correlation (Tibi et al. 2017) and selectivity-based routing (vector
  databases) are stated as known. The remaining claim is their combination for a bounded, zero-padded
  lag scorer on an unmodified approximate inner-product index, the diagnosis and ablation, and the
  integration. The single-traversal lower-bound alternative is answered with the earlier pruning study.
- **Evidence corrections.** Speed-up quoted against the fastest exact path (713 ms, not the historic
  2.9 s scan); per-query minima and queries below 0.9 reported; exact distances distinguished from exact
  candidate selection; float16 probes and HNSW separated from the full-precision identity; measurement
  kinds (experiment, product, historic) labelled; 15-minute load runs not presented as sustained
  operation; jointly changed PostgreSQL settings not attributed individually; background load reported.
- **New analysis.** Why 13 probes at spacing five suffice and seven at spacing ten lose hits: envelope
  autocorrelation on development data (`analysis/grid_spacing.py`). 13 × 50 is labelled an empirically
  chosen configuration.
- **Moved to the supplement** (`supplement.tex`, text unchanged from 0.6): codec and storage studies
  (P2, B1), envelope precision and zero-lag storage (R1b, P1), external and batched builders (CW1),
  access control, recovery and load details, sleep staging and other negative results.
- **Title.** Unchanged for now; alternatives proposed in the PR.

## What changed in draft 0.5

New results since 0.4, all merged on `main`:
- **Faster exact scan (#64):** exact search now runs as one parallel statement, about 2.5× faster with identical results. Noted in the design section and next to R2's exact-scan figures, which describe the original implementation.
- **Filtered search (SS3, #66 and #69), new Section 6.5 and Table 6:**
  - post-filtered probes lost up to half the top ten under selective filters;
  - routing plus filter-aware probes keeps recall at 0.977–1.0, with no filter or RLS violations;
  - the latency verdict was narrowly missed (0.8 ms at the unfiltered level).
- **One stored representation (SS1, #65), new Section 6.6:** exact and saves about 250 MB per 235k windows, but costs 23% exact or 27–32% probe latency. The two-copy layout stays the default.
- **Building representations (CW1, #67 and #68), new Section 6.7:** external workers are exact and crash-safe and move 90% of database CPU. Batching the in-database builder gave a 4.2× cut in database CPU per window, which is most of that benefit.
- **Kept in step:** abstract, design, studies table, limitations, conclusion, evidence ledger and references.
