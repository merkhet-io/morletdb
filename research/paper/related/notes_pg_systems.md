# Literature review: PostgreSQL vector search, extensibility, temporal types, compared with MorletDB

Reviewed 2026-11-01. PDFs are in `scratchpad/papers2/`. Text was extracted with pymupdf, and two MobilityDB pages were rendered to check code listings. Quotes are 15 words or fewer. Page references are the printed page numbers. Where a paper has no page numbers, the reference is a section number.
**Comparability warning.** None of the speedups below predict MorletDB performance. The papers use other datasets (128–1536-d embeddings, 1–10 M vectors), other metrics (QPS at 95% recall@10, CPU cycles inside the index scan), other systems (a commercial PG-compatible engine, PG 13, PG 16, PG 18), and a single-vector ranking score. MorletDB uses a max-over-lags score, 410-d float16 envelopes, 235 k windows, and PG 17.

## 3. Findings per source

### 3.1 VBASE (OSDI'23)
- **Iterator semantics.** HNSW and IVF are re-plumbed to "reuse the traditional Open, Next, and Close interfaces" (§3.2, p.381). For HNSW, `Next` returns "the current closest unvisited node". Visited bitmap and candidate state "will be kept during the query life cycle" (§4.2, p.383). The iterator resumes **within one query**. Nothing survives across queries or calls.
- **Relaxed monotonicity (Def. 1, p.380).** ∃s such that for all t ≥ s, the median distance over the last w steps is ≥ R_q, the radius of the E best seen. For HNSW, "E equals ef" and w = 1 (§3.1, p.381). It is an empirical traversal property ("our observation", p.381). It is not a bound on unseen items.
- **Termination.** It stops "only … if both the original termination condition and relaxed monotonicity check" pass (§3.2, p.381). For ORDER BY … LIMIT K that means K results in the queue *and* phase 2 reached. For range filters it means a distance above R *and* phase 2.
- **What is guaranteed.** The result is equivalent to a TopK-then-filter plan with the oracle-optimal K̃, on the same index and traversal. The paper says: "We rely on the quality of individual indices to ensure recalls" (§3.3, p.382). The proof hinges on "Filter and Sort are commutative" (§3.3, p.383) and assumes "vector index traversal is deterministic" (fn. 1, p.383). It is **not** a guarantee relative to exact search. Reported recalls are 0.96–0.999 against a PG13 brute-force baseline (Table 4, p.388).
- **Composition.** Filters are applied during traversal, before Sort. Multi-column TopK uses NRA over several iterators with a weighted-sum score (§4.4, p.385). Milvus's doubling is criticised: "Every attempt to a larger K is an independent traversal" (p.385).
- **Evaluation context.** Recipe1M subset, 330,922 rows, 1024-d. HNSW M=16, efc=200, ef_search=64. Main table kept in memory. K=50. Single queries on 64 vCPUs. At filter selectivity 0.03, VBASE reaches recall 0.9987 at 34.5 ms avg. PASE with K′=1000 reaches recall 0.5885 (Table 5, p.389). The Volcano path fetches a heap tuple per step and is 2.8% slower than PASE on plain TopK (§5.3, p.387).
- **Carry-over to MorletDB (my analysis, not the paper's).**
  - *Per-tuple predicates* (metadata `@>`, RLS, same rate/channel/unit, source-interval exclusion) commute with Sort, so VBASE's argument applies to them per probe.
  - *Greedy overlap suppression does not commute.* Whether a window survives depends on which overlapping window from the same recording ranked higher. Under an approximately ordered stream, an early-emitted weaker window can later be displaced, so suppression must buffer and retract. "k survivors collected" is therefore not a stable termination test, and the proof step fails.
  - *Max over lags.* Each probe's traversal and termination use the single-lag inner product. D uses the max over 61 lags, and 48 of those lags are never probed. Even with exact sorted access, NRA/TA-style thresholds (max is a monotone aggregator) cannot bound unseen windows on unprobed lags. That would need a bound such as |⟨q_l − q_p, g⟩| ≤ ‖q_l − q_p‖·‖g‖ for the nearest probed lag p. That is only usable if ‖g‖ is bounded or normalised, which needs checking for MorletDB envelopes.
  - *Bottom line.* At most, VBASE-style iteration would remove the fixed LIMIT 50 guess ("oracle-K′ equivalence" per probe). It would not certify recall@k for D.

### 3.2 PostgreSQL-V (CIDR'26)
- **Where time goes (as stated).** Page-based integration means "the memory manager still needed to go through the buffer pool for page indirection" (§2). This is cited to their ICDE'24 study: Faiss beat PASE 2.2–7.3× in search time and 2.9–13.3× in index size. The CIDR paper gives **no executor, heap or buffer breakdown of its own**. The only overhead it quantifies is "constant overhead (around 0.8ms) to parse SQL and retrieve rows" (§4.2).
- **What is decoupled.** Index *contents* live outside PG pages, as native Faiss/hnswlib/DiskANN behind `IndexAmRoutine`. PG keeps the heap, WAL, MVCC visibility, and "status pages" mapping VID→TID (§3.3.1).
- **Ingest and visibility.** It does "not index vectors in memtables". The memtable is a plain array that is scanned (§3.2). The index is built asynchronously on flushed immutable segments. Deletes are soft via per-segment bitmaps at VACUUM. "Visibility checks on result tuples" after the scan keep MVCC correct (§3.3.2).
- **Durability trade-off.** `IndexAmRoutine` "does not expose any transaction-related information" (§3.3). The decoupled index therefore writes no WAL. Uncommitted vectors may be flushed and are invalidated at recovery from the status pages. Recovery took 239 ms for 10 M flushed plus 100 k unflushed vectors. 1,000 kill-and-recover runs showed no inconsistency (§4.4). Physical replication and standbys are **not discussed**. The decoupled index state is not in the WAL stream.
- **Numbers (not transferable).** HNSW 2.9–3.5× and IVF_FLAT 6.5–8.9× faster search than pgvector on SIFT10M/Deep10M with buffers sized to hold the index (§4.2). DiskANN 3.2–4.0× over pgvectorscale with 3 GB buffers and a 39 GB index (§4.3). Recall parity is shown only in figures, which I could not verify from the text.
- **Filtering** is unchanged: "uses the same approach as pgvector" (§3.4). Decoupling therefore does not address MorletDB's selective-filter recall problem.

### 3.3 Filter-agnostic FVS on PostgreSQL (PACMMOD'26)
- **Setup caveats.** The engine is a "commercially available PostgreSQL-compatible system" (§1, 134:3), not stock PG. Filters are precomputed rowID bitmaps. Timing covers the index scan, "excluding external overheads such as bitmap generation and query planning" (§5, 134:14). Prewarmed, shared_buffers 64 GB, 16 client connections, 95% recall@10, HNSW M=32, efc=200.
- **pgvector iterative scan.** It "implements a 'resumable' post-filtering strategy" via a discarded-candidate queue D (§2.3.6, 134:7). They used "the implementation in PGVector 0.8.0" (§3.2, 134:9) and tuned `ef_search` and `max_scan_tuples` per method for best QPS at 95% recall (134:13). They treat post-filtering as subsumed because "Predicting the optimal δ is non-trivial" (§2.1, 134:4), where δ is the overfetch.
- **Correlation matters as much as selectivity.** Under negative correlation at 1% selectivity, "Iterative Scan by 89% relative to the positive correlation" (QPS drop). NaviX drops 53% and ACORN 44%. ScaNN gains 6% (§6.5, 134:21). With positive correlation, traversal-first methods become competitive from about 10% selectivity.
- **Database overhead numbers.** Single-thread CPU share of system overhead (buffer reads, tuple access): NaviX 55.9%, Sweeping 81.8%, ScaNN 84.4%. At 16 threads, cycles per query rise 48–68% (Table 7, 134:22). Without the in-memory indextid→heaptid map, fetching heaptids is "roughly 60%–75% of total cycles" (§6.6.1, 134:21). Database versus HNSWLib latency is "up to 10× higher" (Fig. 1, 134:2).
- **halfvec.** Quantization "yields no consistent QPS improvement": halfvec 0.97–1.04× (§5 and Table 4, 134:12–13). Their reason is that pgvector HNSW is dominated by neighbor page fetches, not bytes. This is directly relevant to MorletDB's halfvec choice.
- **Work counters.** At 1% selectivity, Sweeping does 23.0 K distance computations and 24.0 K page accesses versus NaviX's 246 and 1.2 K (Table 6, 134:15). Iterative scan behaves like Sweeping (§6.2.2).
- **Recommendations.** Algorithms must "expose and minimize system taxes", and the choice is system-aware with no single winner (§7, 134:22–23). Pre-filtering won only at extreme selectivity: "below 0.01% benefiting from pre-filtering" (fn. 1, 134:5). That figure is at 5–10 M rows, so it is not a threshold to copy.
- **Workload generator (method worth copying).** For each query, rank all vectors by distance. Positive correlation samples eligible rows by softmax from the closest third (high) or half (medium). Negative correlation flips the sign (§4.2, 134:11). This lets selectivity and correlation be controlled independently.

### 3.4 Anarchy in the Database (PVLDB'25)
- **Headline.** "16.8% of them are incompatible with at least one other extension" (abstract, p.1962). Failures include crashes, deadlocks (lsm3), shared-memory init errors, and wrong statistics (§5.4, p.1970).
- **pgvector.** Mentioned only as the exemplar of UDT + index access method (p.1963, p.1969) and as a cross-DBMS redundancy example (§6.3, p.1972). **No pgvector compatibility failure is named.** The failures named involve citus, pg_queryid, timescaledb with pg_hint_plan, pg_stat_monitor with pg_stat_statements, lsm3, plprofiler, and others.
- **Hooks.** Failure correlates with "More than 4 Hooks" (p=0.0023) and with planner/utility hooks (Table 7, §5.4, p.1971). "PostgreSQL's hook 'chaining' is the root of many problems" (§6.2, p.1971). PG cannot verify that an extension calls the previous hook.
- **Installation order.** The system "fails to start" unless citus is loaded first or pg_queryid last (§5.4, p.1970). The toolkit "installs them in both permutations" (p.1970).
- **Copied internals and versioning.** 73/441 extensions (16.6%) contain copied PG code, mostly because needed functions are `static`. Duplicate PG code correlates with failures (p=0.0172). 34.7% of extensions use `PG_VERSION_NUM` conditionals (§5.2–5.3, p.1968–1970).
- **Test method.** PG v16, 441 extensions for static analysis, 96 for runtime. A pair counts as compatible if install, the extension's unit tests, and pgbench all pass. UDT × index scripts were generated with Claude 3.5 Sonnet. 29.2% of top-20 failures were "brittle test cases" from pg_regress string diffs (p.1970). Note that §5 says v16, but the cited release-notes reference [18] is v15.3. This is a minor inconsistency in the paper.
- **pgrx** is named as the Rust route for PG extensions (§4, p.1967). The paper has no Rust-specific findings.

### 3.5 MEOS (EDBT'24 demo)
- **Library/DB split.** MEOS gives "a single code base" usable from C, Python, SQL, and streaming (abstract, p.810). It "achieves separation between data storage and processing while maintaining the same functionalities as MobilityDB" (§6, p.813).
- **Types.** tbool, tint, tfloat, tgeompoint/tgeogpoint, `TSequence`, and tbox/stbox (§3.1, p.810–811). Trajectories are built by appending points to a TSequence (§4.1, p.812).
- **"Lossless" compression** "drops points that can be extrapolated" from their neighbours. Records fall 99% but size only 7% (§5, Table 1, p.813). It is lossless only under the interpolation model.
- **Bindings.** PyMEOS has three layers: CFFI, CFFI bindings, and an OO API. It has psycopg/asyncpg connectors to MobilityDB (§3.2, p.811). The demo does not discuss interval-bound conventions. Those are in MobilityDB (§3.6 below).

### 3.6 MobilityDB, TGIS 2022/2023
- **Instants to sequences.** Temporal values are "built from a discrete set of values". Between instants, values are "interpolated using either a stepwise or a linear function" (§3, p.5). A *discrete* trajectory has "no interpolation function" (§1, p.2).
- **Explicit bounds.** Code examples use bracket notation per sequence. `tint '[1@2001-01-01, 1@2001-01-03)'` + `tint '[2@2001-01-02, 2@2001-01-05)'` gives `[3@2001-01-02, 3@2001-01-03)`. `tintersects` returns `{[f@…, t@…, t@…], (f@…, f@…]}` (§3, p.6). Bounds can be inclusive or exclusive on either side, and results split at state changes with complementary open and closed ends.
- **Time types.** `period` "disallows empty periods" and is fixed-length, unlike tstzrange. `periodset` is "ordered and non-overlapping" (§3, p.6). Gaps are represented as periodsets. `timespan` sums intervals "ignoring the gaps between them" (§5.2.1, p.15).
- **Valid and transaction time.** The input has `created` and `timeonserver`, "corresponding to valid time and transaction time" (§4.1, p.7).
- **Infrastructure.** GiST and SP-GiST indexes. Citus multi-node with PG 13, PostGIS 2.5, MobilityDB 1.0 beta3 (§6, p.18–19).

### 3.7 Secondary (one line each)
- **S1 AQP (abstract only).** Simple runtime join switching and LIP in PostgreSQL beat RL-based optimizers in their tests. This supports adding a cheap runtime fallback in MorletDB's router rather than a learned one.
- **S2 PG AIO.** AIO is stock in **PostgreSQL 18** (`io_method` = worker (default), io_uring, or sync). Read streams serve seq and bitmap heap scans. The paper's adaptive lookahead is a proposal evaluated on PG 18. It does not apply to MorletDB on PG 17.
- **S3 RisingWave.** A streaming MV database. Not relevant.
- **S4 MobilityDuck.** MEOS inside DuckDB beats MobilityDB in 13 of 17 BerlinMOD queries. Evidence that the library split pays off across engines. Workload not comparable.

## 4. What these leave unresolved for MorletDB

1. **No recall guarantee for a max-over-lags score with 13 of 61 lags probed.** VBASE guarantees oracle-K′ equivalence for a single-distance ordering with commuting filters. Nothing here bounds the error from unprobed lags. *Action:* report recall loss split into (i) lag-coverage loss, which is exact search restricted to the 13 probed lags versus all 61, and (ii) ANN loss. Test a Cauchy–Schwarz bound if envelopes have bounded norm.
2. **Termination under non-commuting suppression.** No source handles a result-set operator like greedy NMS-within-recording. *Action:* measure the raw-candidates-per-survivor ratio and its tail. Decide whether the 50-per-probe budget can starve k under burst-like matches, where 50%-overlapping windows from one strong match fill a probe.
3. **Negative correlation from source exclusion.** The query's own recording and its overlapping neighbour windows are likely its nearest HNSW neighbours, and the contract excludes them. The filter study shows iterative scan losing 89% of throughput under negative correlation. *Action:* bucket MorletDB benchmark queries by "fraction of each probe's top-50 removed by exclusion/eligibility" and report recall per bucket.
4. **Heap and RLS filter cost is unmeasured.** The filter study's bitmap setup omits the per-candidate heap fetch and RLS predicate MorletDB pays. *Action:* use EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) on probe queries and count heap fetches per returned row.
5. **WAL amplification against the searchable-at-commit contract.** PostgreSQL-V avoids index WAL but gives up WAL-replicated index state and inserts into an unindexed memtable. *Action:* quantify the HNSW WAL share. Evaluate "recent windows via exact scan + deferred HNSW insert" while keeping commit-visibility. State the standby behaviour explicitly.
6. **halfvec benefit unverified.** It is the only pgvector quantization choice MorletDB makes, and the filter study saw no QPS gain from it. *Action:* run a vector versus halfvec ablation at equal recall.
7. **Extension coexistence untested.** pgvector was not implicated in Anarchy, but MorletDB adds a hard dependency on pgvector GUCs (`hnsw.iterative_scan` exists from pgvector 0.8.0) and on RLS. *Action:* run pairwise install-order tests and a pgvector version matrix. Declare `requires = 'vector'` and the minimum version.

## 5. Concepts we may have overlooked

- **Resumable probes with stock pgvector.** A plpgsql cursor over `ORDER BY v <#> probe::halfvec` with no LIMIT, iterative scan on, fetched in batches until k post-suppression survivors stabilise. This is VBASE's `Next` without a new index AM. (pgvector's `hnsw.max_scan_tuples` cap is from pgvector docs, not these papers. Verify it.)
- **Adaptive per-probe budgets.** Spend more on probes whose lag yields better candidates, like VBASE's quality-weighted multi-index traversal (§4.4). Lags near the true alignment will dominate.
- **Range or threshold queries and similarity joins.** VBASE's S4 range filter and Q8 join correspond to "all windows with D < τ" (recurrence or anomaly detection) and "all matches between recording A and recording B". These may be more natural condition-monitoring queries than top-k.
- **Correlation-controlled benchmark.** Adopt the filter study's generator to make selectivity and correlation independent axes instead of reporting selectivity alone.
- **Cost model for the router.** VBASE samples at 0.001 to estimate selectivity (q-error < 1.1) and models per-step cost. MorletDB's fixed T=5000 could be replaced by a cost comparison: eligible windows × exact cost versus probes × expected traversal.
- **Explicit interval semantics.** Following MobilityDB, define window intervals as half-open `[start, start+4096)` in sample offsets. Define what "overlap" means for exclusion and suppression (≥1 shared sample?). Define how gaps and discontinuities between appended chunks are represented: a periodset-like coverage set versus an assumption of contiguity.
- **Valid time and transaction time.** Acquisition time (sample offset / rate + start) is distinct from commit time. Evidence-first and frozen protocols may need "search as of commit LSN/snapshot" for reproducibility.
- **Library split for reproducibility.** A MEOS-style standalone crate for codec, envelope and D, cross-tested against the extension, lets reviewers re-score results outside PostgreSQL.
- **Terminology.** Say "bit-exact lossless". MEOS uses "lossless" for interpolation-reconstructible point dropping.
