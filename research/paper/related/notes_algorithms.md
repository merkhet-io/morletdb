# Prior-art check, topic A: algorithmic prior art for shifted-probe bounded-lag correlation search

Compiled 2026-11-01. "FULL TEXT" means I downloaded the PDF, converted it to text, and read the relevant sections. "PARTIAL" means I grepped and read targeted passages. "ABSTRACT/SECONDARY" means I saw only the abstract, a search snippet, a project page, or I am relying on prior knowledge of the paper. Treat ABSTRACT/SECONDARY rows as unverified.

Our method, for reference: d(q,g) = 1 − max_{|lag|≤30} (1/n) Σ q[t+lag] g[t]. Each window (n=410) is z-scored once, and the correlation is biased with zero padding (this is NCC_b in Paparrizos & Gravano's terms, restricted to a bounded lag). Candidate generation sends 13 query-side shifted, zero-padded probes into one ordinary inner-product HNSW index (pgvector halfvec), which holds a single copy of each stored vector. The candidates are unioned and then re-ranked exactly with the full-lag scorer. The search runs inside Postgres, returns (recording, interval), and supports filters.

Note on metric structure: z-scored windows all have norm √n, so on the stored side MIPS, cosine and L2 give the same ranking. Each shifted probe is therefore an ordinary NN query. Because of the zero padding, bounded-lag d is not a pseudo-metric in general, so triangle-inequality pruning (Yankov et al.) does not transfer directly.

## 2. Per-work notes

**Keogh et al. 2006/2009, LB_Keogh for rotation invariance** (VLDB 2006, pp. 882–893; VLDB J. 18:611–630, 2009, DOI 10.1007/s00778-008-0111-4). FULL TEXT of the expanded 2006 version. For a query shape C, they build "a n by n matrix containing every rotation of C". The database Q stays unchanged: "the rest of the database uses the original (arbitrary rotation) time series, and since the size of the database is assumed to be large, this overhead is asymptotically irrelevant." In the 2006 text the role of the letters is swapped: C is the query and Q is the database. Rotation-limited queries are supported explicitly: "Find the best match to this shape allowing a maximum rotation of ±15 degrees. Our framework trivially supports such rotation-limited queries, by removing from the matrix C all time series that correspond to the unwanted rotations." Mirror invariance works the same way, by adding reversed rows. The query rotations are clustered hierarchically into K wedges (upper/lower envelopes over groups of similar rotations, which are typically adjacent shifts). Each database sequence is compared to the wedges with LB_Keogh, and a wedge is opened only when its bound fails (H-Merge). The result is exact. For disk-scale indexing (§4.2), a VP-tree is built on an FFT-magnitude lower bound (rotation invariant), and survivors are re-ranked with H-Merge. The authors also note that the problem becomes "search for the best match to K envelopes", i.e. K query-side objects against one index. The distance is ED (also DTW and LCSS) on shape time series that are normalized for offset and scale. Shifts are **circular**, not zero-padded. **What it contains of our idea:** query-side materialization of the transformed query set; one copy of the data; bounded ("rotation-limited") transformation sets; grouping neighbouring transformations so that a few probe objects stand in for many lags (wedges ≈ our sparse probe grid); a candidate filter followed by exact re-rank. **What it lacks:** inner-product/correlation formulation with zero-padded linear lags; use of an off-the-shelf ANN (graph) index; one independent ANN query per probe with a union; an approximate recall-based design and the measured failure of a zero-lag shortlist. The bound used at index level (FFT magnitude) covers all rotations, so the bounded case is pruned only loosely there.

**Yankov et al. 2007** (SDM; UCR PDF). Proves that rotation-invariant ED is a pseudo-metric and uses triangle-inequality pivot pruning (Burkhard-Keller-style) over rotations. Rotations are full and circular, and the search is exact. This does not transfer to our zero-padded bounded-lag score, which is not shown to be a metric.

**Vlachos et al. 2005** (CIKM, pp. 131–138, DOI 10.1145/1099554.1099580). Rotation becomes a circular shift along the Hough θ axis. They make it invariant by keeping only Fourier **magnitudes** and discarding phase, then compress the top-k coefficients with an error bound and index with a VPC-tree. This is the canonical **data-side invariant feature** approach. It covers full circular shifts only and cannot express a bounded lag or zero-padded edges. It is a contrast case, not an overlap.

**Paparrizos & Gravano, k-Shape/SBD** (SIGMOD 2015; TODS 42(2), 2017). SBD = 1 − max over all 2m−1 shifts of NCC_c via FFT. The TODS paper explicitly defines NCC_b (biased, divide by m), which is our per-lag statistic on z-scored data, and NCC_u. There is no index and no bounded lag (SBD uses all shifts). The 1-NN speedup comes from cluster centroids (NSC), not from vector indexing. It is the source of our scorer family but has no overlap with our candidate generation.

**GRAIL** (PVLDB 12(11):1762–1777, 2019). Learns a Nyström embedding for SINK, a soft-max-over-all-circular-shifts kernel. Similarity in the embedding approximates the shift-invariant kernel, and GRAIL-LB gives pruning. This is the strongest "learned correlation space" prior work. It works on the data side, uses full circular shifts, gives approximate answers, and relies on its own LB rather than a general ANN index. It does not use query-side probes.

**MASS / Matrix Profile / UCR Suite.** These are exact scans. They z-normalize **every subsequence**, which makes them equivalent to max Pearson over all offsets. They have no index and no bounded lag. They differ from us both in normalization (per subsequence versus once per window with zero padding) and in mechanism (scan versus ANN).

**Foulds et al. 2015** (Discrete Appl. Math. 197:123–144, DOI 10.1016/j.dam.2015.02.011). From the snippet only: kNN where the distance is the minimum over cyclic permutations of the **query**, with the goal of reducing it to standard kNN. That is conceptually close to "max over transformed queries → standard NN", but it uses full cyclic permutations and image descriptors, and the reported transformation is expensive. I could not read the full text, so treat this as a possible overlap that needs checking.

**ColBERT end-to-end retrieval** (SIGIR 2020, arXiv 2004.12832). Verbatim mechanism: "we concurrently issue N_q vector-similarity queries … onto our faiss index … retrieves the top-k′ … producing N_q × k′ document IDs, only K ≤ N_q × k′ of which are unique … we refine this set by exhaustively re-ranking only those K documents". This is our exact candidate-generation pattern: several query vectors, one ANN index, union, exact re-rank of a max-based late-interaction score. The several vectors are token embeddings, not transformations of one query. The score is sum of max, and the documents are multi-vector. It is strong precedent for the systems pattern, not for the shift identity.

**Neural audio fingerprint** (Chang et al., ICASSP 2021). MIPS (faiss) over 1 s segment embeddings with 0.5 s hop. Shift robustness is **learned** through ±200 ms offset augmentation and helped by data-side overlap. Sequence search sends each query segment to the index, compensates the offsets, unions the candidates and re-scores. This is multi-query union + re-rank with temporal offsets, but the offsets index different query segments, not shifted copies of the same window.

**FAST** (Yoon et al. 2015). Waveform similarity search in seismology, the closest application domain. Shift tolerance comes from **data-side** dense overlapping windows plus robust binary fingerprints, with Jaccard similarity via MinHash LSH. It does not optimize cross-correlation, and precise CC is left to post-processing.

**Multi-probe LSH** (Lv et al. 2007). The probes perturb bucket codes of one query, not the query signal itself. It is analogous in name only and gives no max-over-transformations reduction.

**ALSH / cone trees (MIPS).** These cover MIPS-to-NN reductions with asymmetric transforms. Our z-scored stored vectors have constant norm, so MIPS reduces to cosine trivially. Neither work addresses max over a set of query transforms.

**Hydra 2 benchmark; iSAX/MESSI/Hercules/Dumpy; SEAnet.** All target whole-series (or subsequence) z-normalized ED or DTW. In the Hydra 2 text I found no treatment of shift or correlation invariance (grep for shift/rotation/invariance gave no relevant hits). None of them supports bounded-lag correlation directly. One could index shifted copies data-side, at L-fold cost.

**BRAID / StatStream.** These monitor lag correlations between streams, and BRAID samples lags geometrically. Sampling lags is related to our sparse probe grid, but the problem is pairwise stream monitoring, not k-NN retrieval.

## 3. Strongest overlap (ranked)

1. **Keogh et al. VLDB 2006 / VLDBJ 2009 (rotation-invariant shape indexing with wedges).** Covers: query-side expansion into the set of transformed queries against an unchanged, singly stored database. Bounded ("rotation-limited") transformation sets. Grouping neighbouring transformations into a few probe objects (wedges), which is the closest analogue of our 13-of-61 sparse grid. Filter then exact re-rank. Does not cover: zero-padded linear lags or the inner-product identity; an approximate ANN (HNSW) backend probed once per transformed query with a union; recall-calibrated sparse probing without guarantees; the empirical finding that a zero-lag shortlist misses ~26% of bounded-lag top-10; and in-DB traceable intervals and filters. Their index-level bound (FFT magnitude) is for full rotations. The paper must be cited, and its query-side framing should be acknowledged as prior.
2. **ColBERT end-to-end retrieval (Khattab & Zaharia 2020).** Covers the exact systems pattern: several query vectors → independent ANN searches on one index → union of IDs → exact re-rank under a max-based score. Does not cover: the query vectors being group transformations of one signal; the max-over-lags = max-over-shifted-queries identity; time series.
3. **GRAIL/SINK (Paparrizos & Franklin 2019)**, tied with **Foulds et al. 2015** (unverified). GRAIL covers indexable, vector-space approximation of shift-invariant cross-correlation similarity, but on the data side, with full circular shifts and a learned embedding. Foulds et al. appear to reduce kNN under min over cyclic query permutations to standard kNN, which needs a full-text check. Neither covers bounded zero-padded lags with sparse query probes on a stock ANN index.

Honourable mention: Chang et al. 2021 (multi-segment MIPS union with offset compensation, learned shift robustness) and FAST (data-side overlapping windows for waveform search).

**Bottom line:** the idea of rotating or shifting the query instead of the data, including restricting the set to a bounded range, is clearly prior art (Keogh 2006). Union of ANN results over several query vectors followed by exact re-rank is also prior art (ColBERT). I did **not** find a work that combines them, i.e. a bounded zero-padded cross-correlation expressed as a max of inner products over shifted queries, served by a sparse grid of query-side probes into a single stock inner-product ANN index, with the failure of a zero-lag shortlist measured. Not finding it is not proof that it does not exist. The defensible claim is the combination plus the empirical analysis, not query-side shifting itself.
