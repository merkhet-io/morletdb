## 2. Per-work notes

**FAST (Yoon, O'Reilly, Bergen, Beroza, "Earthquake detection through computationally efficient similarity search", Science Advances 1(11):e1501057, 2015, doi:10.1126/sciadv.1501057).** Read the full text via EuropePMC PMC4672764. FAST computes a spectrogram (10 s windows, 0.1 s lag), cuts it into 10 s spectral images with a 1 s lag (the fingerprint sampling period), applies a Haar wavelet, MAD normalization and top-k selection, and binarizes the result. It then runs a MinHash-LSH all-pairs search on Jaccard similarity. Shift tolerance comes from the data side: fingerprints overlap densely (1 s lag on 10 s windows, so each sample sits in about 10 fingerprints), and the smoothed spectral features are robust to sub-lag misalignment. There is no explicit lag maximization. Search is approximate. Results are fingerprint-index pairs, i.e. times, and the paper counts events as matching if they fall within 19 s of each other. Overlap with MorletDB: a robust compact representation of dense windows, approximate candidates, reporting in time. Differences: no exact re-rank with a CC scorer, no query-side shifts, data-side replication, batch self-join, no DB, filters or transactions.

**Rong, Yoon, Bergen, Elezabi, Bailis, Levis, Beroza, "Locality-sensitive hashing for earthquake detection: a case study of scaling data-driven science", PVLDB 11(11):1674–1687, 2018.** Read the full text. It is FAST plus systems engineering: MAD via sampling, Min-Max hash, LSH partitioning for out-of-core runs, and spatiotemporal alignment (channel-sum, then diagonal clustering at a constant offset Δt, removal of self-matches from overlapping fingerprints). The 1 s lag gives about 300M fingerprints for 10 years, and the hash signatures take about 250 GB (100 tables × 64-bit). The partitioned search gives the same results as the unpartitioned search, but LSH itself is approximate. This is a strong data point for the storage cost of data-side dense windowing plus multi-table LSH, compared with our single index of non-overlapping windows. No updates, filters, or exact re-rank.

**Bergen & Beroza, "Detecting earthquakes over a seismic network using single-station similarity measures", GJI 213(3), 2018, doi:10.1093/gji/ggy100; Bergen, Yoon & Beroza 2016 (LNCS, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-46759-7_23).** Metadata only. These extend FAST to network-level association. Bergen & Beroza 2019 PAGEOPH ("Earthquake fingerprints…", doi believed to be 10.1007/s00024-018-1995-6) was NOT verified.

**EQcorrscan (Chamberlain et al., SRL 89(1), 2018, doi:10.1785/0220170151); FMF (Beaucé, Frank & Romanenko, SRL 89(1), 2018, doi:10.1785/0220170181); Match & Locate (Zhang & Wen, GJI 200(3), 2015, doi:10.1093/gji/ggu466).** Checked the docs/README and Crossref metadata; the algorithmic description is from memory. All three do exact normalized cross-correlation of a template slid over continuous data at every sample, i.e. full shift and brute force, stacked across channels (M&L also stacks over trial-location moveouts, which amounts to many query-side shifted versions). EQcorrscan's lag_calc uses a bounded shift (shift_len ±0.2 s by default) to refine picks after detection: CC maximized over a bounded lag around a known alignment. That is the closest analogue to our |lag| ≤ 30 scorer, but it is a re-picking step, not retrieval. None of them has an index, filters, or transactional storage.

**Tibi, Young, Gonzales, Ballard, Encarnacao, "Rapid and robust cross-correlation-based seismic signal identification using an approximate nearest neighbor method", BSSA 107(4), 2017, doi:10.1785/0120170011.** I could not reach OSTI and saw only Crossref metadata and a search snippet. ANN over reduced-dimension waveform windows to approximate CC template matching, reported as about 25x faster than linear search with recall above 80%. This is potentially a very close conceptual precedent (ANN candidates standing in for CC), and whether it does an exact CC re-rank and how it handles shifts must be checked in the full text.

**Naoi & Hirano, GJI 237:604–621, 2024, doi:10.1093/gji/ggae061.** Abstract only. Learned 64-bit hash codes for about 35M moving windows, Hamming search, compared with correlation. Data-side windows, approximate.

**Audio fingerprinting: Wang, "An industrial-strength audio search algorithm", ISMIR 2003 (memory); Panako, Six & Leman ISMIR 2014 and Six ISMIR 2021 (README checked).** Offset tolerance is unbounded: landmark hashes are offset-invariant, postings store absolute times, and a match is a consistent offset (Δt histogram). Results give query and reference start/stop seconds, so they trace back to an interval of the original recording, which is prior art for interval-shaped hits. Panako stores postings in LMDB with inserts. Not a CC scorer, no bounded shift, no transactions or filters.

**KV-match (Wu, Wang, Pan, Wang, Wang, Wang, ICDE 2019; arXiv 1710.00560).** Read the full text. Constrained normalized subsequence matching (cNSM): z-normalized ED or DTW with constraints α (amplitude scaling) and β (offset shifting). It indexes the means of all sliding windows in a KV store (HBase or files), splits the query into disjoint windows, intersects window intervals, and verifies exactly with no false dismissals. Index size is about 10% of the data. Hits are subsequence offsets in the stored series. This is the strongest DB-side precedent for exact, normalized, offset-addressed retrieval with index cost reported relative to the raw data. Differences: no bounded lag (it matches every offset exactly), no transactional appends, no filters, no ANN.

**ULISSE (Linardi & Palpanas, "Scalable, variable-length similarity search in data series: the ULISSE approach", PVLDB 11(13):2236–2248, 2018, doi:10.14778/3275366.3275372; VLDBJ 2020, arXiv 2009.10373).** Read the full text. One envelope (min/max PAA bounds) covers γ consecutive offsets ("master series") and a range of lengths. Exact and approximate k-NN under ED or DTW, raw or Z-normalized, with lower-bound pruning. This is the canonical answer to "don't store one entry per shift": shift redundancy is compressed on the data side and γ trades index size against tightness. We instead put the shifts on the query side (13 probes) over one stored copy per window. Static index, no DB transactions, no filters.

**ModelarDB (Jensen, Pedersen, Thomsen, PVLDB 11(11):1688–1701, 2018); TimescaleDB, InfluxDB, SciDB, TileDB, WFDB/PhysioNet, ObsPy/FDSN.** From memory, not re-verified. These provide time-range read-back of raw or bounded-error data, with metadata predicates and append ingest, but no integrated waveform similarity search that returns intervals. TileDB-Vector-Search exists as a separate library and was not checked.

**pgvector (github.com/pgvector/pgvector README, checked).** Exact search by default. HNSW/IVF filter after the index scan. Since 0.8.0, iterative index scans (strict_order / relaxed_order, hnsw.max_scan_tuples = 20000). The README advises exact indexes (B-tree) for conditions matching a low percentage of rows and partial indexes for a few distinct filter values, and notes that a table scan may be faster for small tables. In other words, PostgreSQL's cost-based planner already routes between an exact (B-tree filter + sort) plan and an HNSW plan. pgvector is WAL-logged and inherits ACID. Our "filter-aware probe search (iterative index scan)" uses this feature directly.

**VBase (Zhang et al., "VBASE: Unifying online vector similarity search and relational queries via relaxed monotonicity", OSDI 2023).** Read the full text. Volcano-style iterator over HNSW/IVF/SPANN with a relaxed-monotonicity termination check, implemented in PostgreSQL. It has a cost model for vector index scans plus sampling-based selectivity estimates (sample rate 0.001, q-error < 1.1), so the planner can choose between index scans and other plans. This is the academic basis of iterative filtered index scans in PG.

**AnalyticDB-V (Wei et al., PVLDB 13(12):3152–3165, 2020).** Read the full text. Plan A runs a B-tree filter and then exact brute force, and is described as working well "on small datasets (e.g., thousands of points)". It is also the conservative default when the approximate plans cannot meet the accuracy requirement. Plans B/C (PQ / VGPQ with a bitmap) and D (ANN first, then filter) are chosen by an accuracy-aware cost model. Lambda architecture: HNSW on incremental data and VGPQ on baseline data, merged asynchronously. Read/write decoupling "trades consistency for low query latency". **This is direct prior art for exact/approximate routing by filtered-set size.**

**Milvus (Wang et al., SIGMOD 2021, doi:10.1145/3448016.3457550) and Knowhere source.** Read the full text and the code. Strategy A (attribute-first, then full scan) is "suitable when C_A is highly selective" and "produces the exact results". Strategy D is cost-based (citing ADBV), and E is partition-based. In current Knowhere HNSW code, IndexConditionalWrapper.cc switches to brute force when filtered_out ≥ 0.93·N (kNN) or ≥ 0.97 (range/iterator), or when k ≥ 0.5·survivors. Freshness: an LSM MemTable flushed every second or by size; writes are asynchronous ("users may not immediately see the inserted data" unless flush()); snapshot isolation.

**Weaviate docs.** flatSearchCutoff (default 40000) switches to brute force over the allow-list when the filter is restrictive. ACORN is the default filter strategy since v1.34. A dynamic index starts flat and converts to HNSW at 10000 objects (one way).

**Qdrant docs.** full_scan_threshold: when the estimated cardinality of the filtered points is below the threshold, the planner uses a payload index plus full rescore instead of HNSW. Filterable HNSW adds extra edges for indexed payload values.

**Vespa schema reference.** approximate-threshold (current default 0.02): if the estimated filter hit ratio is below it, Vespa runs an exact NN search with pre-filtering. filter-first-threshold is 0.2 and post-filter-threshold defaults to 1.0 (disabled).

**Lucene (AbstractKnnVectorQuery.getLeafResults, read in code) / Elasticsearch / OpenSearch.** Lucene: if the filter cost is ≤ perLeafTopK, it runs exact search. Otherwise HNSW runs with visitedLimit = cost+1, and if that limit is exceeded it falls back to exact search. Lucene 10 adds KnnSearchStrategy.Hnsw filteredSearchThreshold. OpenSearch Faiss: the index-level knn.advanced.filtered_exact_search_threshold, plus a fallback to exact search when fewer than k results are returned.

**ACORN (Patel, Kraft, Guestrin, Zaharia, SIGMOD 2024 / PACMMOD 2(2), doi:10.1145/3654923; arXiv 2403.04871).** Read the full text. It uses a "simple cost-based model during search": if the estimated selectivity is > 1/γ it searches ACORN-γ, otherwise it pre-filters (brute force). The paper notes that a misestimate costs speed, not recall, in the pre-filter direction. This is a clean academic statement of selectivity-based exact/approximate routing.

**Filtered-DiskANN (Gollapudi et al., WWW 2023, doi:10.1145/3543507.3583552); FreshDiskANN (Singh et al., arXiv:2105.09613, 2021).** Metadata/abstract only. Label-aware graphs; streaming inserts and deletes with stable recall, using a delta plus a periodic merge.

**SingleStore-V (Chen, Jin, Zhang, Podolsky et al., PVLDB 17(12), 2024).** Read the full text. One ANN index per immutable columnstore segment, plus an exact full scan of the unindexed in-memory rowstore. Search sees an MVCC snapshot over both, and changes are visible at commit ("full update, delete, and ACID transaction support"). Filters are pushed into a Top(m, dist, filters) operator: the batch size starts at c·m (1/c = estimated selectivity) and doubles until enough rows pass. This is the strongest precedent for "searchable at commit with transactional consistency". It avoids synchronous index maintenance by brute-forcing the fresh delta, which contrasts with our in-transaction HNSW maintenance and its WAL amplification.

**VStream (Gong et al., PVLDB 18(6):1593–1606, 2025).** Abstract only. Vector search embedded in a streaming engine, with hot/cold tiers and a dynamic partitioner. Not transactional in the DB sense per the abstract.

## 3. Strongest overlap (ranked)

1. **SingleStore-V (PVLDB 2024)**, with **AnalyticDB-V / Milvus / ACORN / Vespa / Weaviate / Qdrant / Lucene** for routing. SingleStore-V covers vector search inside a transactional RDBMS, visibility at commit, MVCC-consistent results, filters with adaptive iterative over-fetch, and exact scan of fresh data. It does not cover waveforms, raw-chunk read-back, bounded-shift scoring, query-side shifts, or in-transaction HNSW maintenance. Our router (exact for small eligible galleries, ≤5000 windows; ANN otherwise) is **not new**: AnalyticDB-V Plan A ("thousands of points"), Milvus Strategy A/D and Knowhere's 93% threshold, Weaviate flatSearchCutoff = 40000, Qdrant full_scan_threshold, Vespa approximate-threshold = 0.02, Lucene cost ≤ k → exact, and ACORN s_min = 1/γ all do exact/approximate routing by filtered cardinality. Cite them and claim only the specific threshold choice and our measurements.
2. **KV-match (ICDE 2019)**, with **ULISSE (PVLDB 2018)**. These cover DB/KV-store-backed exact subsequence retrieval under z-normalization, with hits as offsets in the original series, compact index size (about 10% of the data for KV-match), and data-side compression of shift redundancy (ULISSE γ-envelopes). They do not cover CC over a bounded lag, query-side shifted probes into an ANN index, transactional appends, metadata/RLS filters, or lossless chunk storage.
3. **FAST / Rong et al. 2018 (PVLDB)**, with **Tibi et al. 2017 BSSA**. These cover similarity search over dense windows of continuous seismic data with compact fingerprints and approximate candidate generation, reported as times (diagonal offset clustering), plus concrete storage numbers for data-side replication (1 s lag, 10 s windows, 100 LSH tables ≈ 250 GB for 10 yr). Tibi et al. use ANN to accelerate CC template matching, which may include an exact CC check (unverified). They do not cover query-side shift probes, a single stored copy per window, exact CC re-rank with bounded lag (FAST uses Jaccard), a DB, transactions, or filters.

Also relevant: the bounded-lag CC scorer resembles EQcorrscan lag_calc (shift_len), which uses bounded lag only for refinement. Query-side multi-shift stacking appears in Match & Locate (exact brute force, no index). Iterative filtered index scans in PostgreSQL come from pgvector 0.8 / VBase. Our use of them is engineering, not novelty.

What appears not covered by any single work I checked (not proof of novelty): putting bounded-lag shift tolerance on the query side as a fixed set of zero-padded shifted probes against a single-copy inner-product HNSW index, followed by exact bounded-lag CC re-rank; combined with transactional, searchable-at-commit maintenance next to losslessly coded raw chunks, and bit-exact interval read-back.
